# SIH 26055 — clause-by-clause compliance

*Development of Smart Scan Strategy for Electronic Warfare in the absence of
prior reliable intelligence of emitters and their operating characteristics.*

Every requirement in the problem statement, the code that answers it, and the
evidence. Numbers are regenerated by `python scripts/figures_of_merit.py` and
live in [`reports/figures_of_merit.md`](../reports/figures_of_merit.md).

## 1. Figures of merit

The PS names seven. `smartscan/analysis/metrics.py` numbers them in its
docstrings (`**Metric 1**` … `**Metric 10**`) so each is greppable.

| PS wording | Implementation | Value (medium tier) |
|---|---|---|
| probability of detection | `empirical_pd` — per SNR bin, Clopper-Pearson intervals | 1.000 at high SNR |
| probability of false alarm | `empirical_pfa` — noise-only channels | 0.00 (7.6e-05 on easy) |
| sensitivity | `sensitivity_db` — min SNR for Pd ≥ 0.9 at Pfa 1e-3, Swerling cases | 18.1 dB pulse / 2.9 dB energy |
| avg intercept rate | `average_intercept_rate` | 137.8 /s |
| avg reward / cost function | `average_reward` — discounted and undiscounted | 209.1 |
| percentage of correct predictions | `prediction_scores` → `accuracy` | 94.31 % |
| average intercept time error | `average_intercept_time_error` | 0.142 s |

**On "percentage of correct predictions".** Reported, with the caveat that
matters: occupancy is ~8.6 % positive, so an always-idle model scores **91.37 %**
on the same test set. The real model's 94.31 % is three points above a model that
does nothing. Average precision is quoted beside it (0.531, a **6.15×** lift over
the base rate) because AP collapses to the base rate for the degenerate model and
accuracy does not. The scheduler ranks channels by predicted occupancy and never
thresholds, so accuracy describes an operating point it does not use.

## 2. System model

| Requirement | Where |
|---|---|
| receiver system model | `smartscan/env/receiver.py` — IBW, retune settle cost, dwell |
| measurements from a simulated RF environment | `smartscan/env/rf_environment.py`, `smartscan/hal/simulated.py` |
| truth on emitter status per band per time slot | `EpisodeTensors.occupancy` `(B, T)`, plus `snr_db` and `truth_ids` |
| spectrum of many bands | 0.5–18 GHz; receiver sees 1/32 of the band per dwell |
| status recorded as transmission / non-transmission | binary occupancy tensor; `Observation` carries hits, and `pfa_flags` for evaluation only |

The belief state (`smartscan/agents/belief.py`) is built **only** from
`Observation`. It never reads ground truth, and deliberately does not read
`pfa_flags` — so no scheduler can cheat by seeing which detections were false.

## 4. Primary objective — the ML scheduler

> *"Development of a robust scheduler using machine learning to minimize
> intercept time and ensure a high interception rate."*
> *Expected Solution: Machine learning based Electronic Support receiver
> scheduler software.*

Four learning-based schedulers were built, trained and benchmarked:

| Scheduler | Learning method | Trained on |
|---|---|---|
| `whittle` | **Restless multi-armed bandit.** Online Bayesian belief updated from hits and misses; Whittle index computed per channel per slot | learns **during** the mission |
| `predictor` | Transformer occupancy model, teacher-student distillation | 3 tiers × supervised occupancy |
| `dqn` | Deep Q-network | 198 K – 3 M env steps |
| `ppo` | Proximal policy optimisation | 198 K – 3 M env steps |
| `hybrid` | Predictor features + learned policy head | 598 K – 3 M env steps |

**The headline result is `whittle`, and it is a machine-learning scheduler in
the sense the PS requires** — it holds no prior intelligence about any emitter,
and every quantity it acts on is estimated online from its own hits and misses.
Restless bandits are a reinforcement-learning formalism; the belief update is
discounted-Bayesian tracking (Garivier & Moulines; Raj & Kalyani). This is the
right class of answer for *"absence of prior reliable intelligence"*: a model
trained offline encodes exactly the prior intelligence the PS says you do not
have.

### The offline deep-RL agents lose, and we report it

Across 30 seeds per tier, on emitters never intercepted (lower is better):

| | easy | medium | hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| best hand-designed / online | 0 | 61 (`coprime_sweep`) | 138 (`sequential`) |
| best offline-trained (`dqn`) | 0 | 77 | 181 |
| `ppo` | 8 | 78 | 484 |
| `hybrid` | 26 | 102 | 803 |

No offline-trained agent beats the untrained baseline on any tier. This is not a
training-budget artefact: `dqn_hard`'s return climbed from **−570 to +312** over
3 M steps — it learned its objective well — and still missed more emitters than a
plain sweep. The reward it maximises (threat-weighted intercepts on emitters
already found) is not the metric it is judged on (distinct emitters *ever*
found). We report this rather than hide it, because it is the substantive
finding: **for this problem, learning online beats learning offline.**

### Losing time to nonthreatening emitters — the PS's complaint, measured

> *"Open loop strategies focus only on this requirement and **may lose time to
> nonthreatening emitters** by not giving time to new or threatening ones."*

The `hard` tier makes this cost real: 5 of 30 emitters are interferers and
`w5_interferer_dwell` is doubled to 2.0, so every dwell spent on a decoy is
charged. That drives **every** policy's total return negative on `hard`, which
makes the differences between them the thing to read rather than the sign.

Dwells wasted on decoys, hard tier, 8 seeds:

| Scheduler | mean return | mean decoy dwells | vs open-loop sweep | seeds won |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `phase_locked` | −380.6 | **389.4** | **−58.1 %** | 8/8 |
| `whittle` | −509.5 | **448.4** | **−51.8 %** | 8/8 |
| `coprime_sweep` | −652.2 | 514.6 | −44.7 % | 8/8 |
| `sequential` (open loop) | −1455.8 | 930.0 | — | — |

The closed-loop schedulers waste roughly **half** as much time on nonthreatening
emitters as the open-loop sweep, on every seed tested. This is the problem
statement's stated failure mode of open-loop scanning, quantified, and it is the
metric on which the closed-loop advantage is largest.

Per-term decomposition of the return, regenerated with the table:
[`reports/figures_of_merit.md`](../reports/figures_of_merit.md). Staleness
contributes under a point — it is not what drives the sign.

## 5. "Approaches to intercept a periodic scan receiver optimally"

`smartscan/analysis/scan_on_scan.py` answers this deterministically rather than
probabilistically, which is the part most treatments get wrong.

Two periodic processes do not intercept with probability *p* per look. Writing
the relative phase after *n* sweeps as `ψ_n = (n·Tr − φ) mod Te`, an intercept
occurs iff `ψ_n` lands in a window of width `wr + we`. Then:

- **Incommensurate `Tr/Te`** — `ψ_n` is equidistributed (Weyl), intercept is
  certain, and `E[TTI] ≈ Tr·Te / (wr + we)`. Gap structure follows the
  **three-distance theorem**: at most three distinct gap lengths, set by the
  continued-fraction convergents of `Tr/Te`.
- **Commensurate `Tr/Te = p/q`** — the phase visits only *q* points and can
  **lock out permanently**.

Measured, for a 4.0 s / 2° scanner:

| `Tr` | `Tr/Te` | initial phases that **never** intercept | classical `E[TTI]` claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.096 s | 1/32 | 27 % | 16.5 s |
| 1.000 s | **1/4** | **97.7 %** | 172 s |
| 2.000 s | **1/2** | **98.8 %** | 344 s |

The textbook closed form reports a finite mean for cases where 98.8 % of
encounters never occur. A uniform sweep has exactly one revisit period, so it is
the policy *most* exposed to this. US Patent 6,020,842 exists because fielded
ESM sets hit it.

**Our answer:** a golden-ratio Weyl sequence (`phase_locked`) — the
worst-approximable irrational, which by the three-distance theorem minimises the
largest phase gap for every prefix length. Largest gap at N = 60: **0.034**
(golden) against **0.500** (½-periodic). A deterministic guarantee where duty
dithering gives only an expectation.

Scan periods are recovered online by Lomb-Scargle and CDIF/SDIF
(`analysis/estimators.py`): **0.17 %** median relative error against ground
truth, 26 of 40 emitters resolved, arrival-time error 0.142 s.

## 6. Dataset

`JC Wise, Radar emitter Database, 2024` —
`alan-turing-institute/turing-synthetic-radar-dataset`.

Bridged in `smartscan/data/tsrd_bridge.py`; results in
`reports/external_tsrd.json` and `reports/external_tsrd_scan.json` (78 emitters,
29 748 PDWs over 9.54 s, 0.36–12.00 GHz).

Reported **separately** from the synthetic benchmarks and not pooled with them:
the PDW-to-occupancy binning is an assumption of the bridge, the emitter mix is
whatever TSRD contains rather than our tiers, and TSRD carries no threat model,
so every emitter is scored at uniform priority. The dataset is Apache-2.0 but
access-gated, so it is fetched at runtime with the user's own token and never
mirrored into this repository.
