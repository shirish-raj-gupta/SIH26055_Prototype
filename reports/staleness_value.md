# What a revisit is actually worth

_Generated by `scripts/staleness_value.py` from 8,261,920 replayed dwells._

Every value-based scheduler scores a channel as `P(occupied) + coverage_weight x staleness`,
with the staleness term a straight line and `coverage_weight` fixed at 1.0 since
it was written. This measures the shape that line approximates.

**The revisit gap has to be exogenous for this to mean anything.** For a
belief-driven scheduler the gap is *chosen*: a long gap means the policy decided
the channel was dead, so a low hit rate at long gaps is partly that decision
rather than staleness. Only `sequential` and `random` pick their gaps without
reading a hit, so their pooled curve identifies the causal shape and the rest
are shown beside it to expose how large the selection effect is.

## Is it flat?

Chi-square for homogeneity of `P(hit)` across gap bins, per tier, for each
open-loop scheduler separately -- separately because pooling them is what
manufactured a spurious effect the first time this was run.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `easy` | `random` (primary) | 0.0343 | 15 | 1,991,755 | 9.3 | 0.809 | flat |
| `hard` | `sequential` | 0.0742 | 5 | 644,544 | 135.4 | 2.68e-28 | **not flat** |
| `hard` | `random` (primary) | 0.0750 | 13 | 358,163 | 13.8 | 0.312 | flat |
| `medium` | `sequential` | 0.0540 | 5 | 3,007,872 | 11.1 | 0.0251 | **not flat** |
| `medium` | `random` (primary) | 0.0538 | 14 | 1,672,717 | 11.7 | 0.551 | flat |

`random` is the instrument the conclusion rests on. Both schedulers pick
gaps without reading a hit, but a saw-tooth only ever produces gap 1 and its
own revisit period, so its long-gap bins hold a few hundred looks out of
hundreds of thousands and are edge artifacts. Uniform draws populate every bin.
