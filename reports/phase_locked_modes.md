# `phase_locked`: which fallback, and what it costs

_Generated by `scripts/phase_locked_modes.py`._

`phase_locked` parks on a predicted beam arrival and runs a fallback policy the
rest of the time. That is most of the episode, so the fallback decides most of
the score. Both modes ship; neither is universally better.

Relative to the `whittle` default, over paired seeds per tier:

| tier | intercept time | interception rate | never intercepted | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `easy` | -83.6% | -49.2% | +0 | **dominated** — worse at everything |
| `medium` | +11.2% | -68.1% | -7 | Pareto trade |
| `hard` | +19.8% | -66.8% | -17 | Pareto trade |

**The default stays `whittle`.** Interception rate is an objective the problem
statement names; never-intercepted is a diagnostic this project added. A change
that gives up two thirds of the collection rate to improve a diagnostic is not
an upgrade, and on `easy` the sweep fallback is simply worse at everything --
coverage is already free there, both modes reach zero never-intercepted, so
slots spent sweeping buy nothing.

**Use `coprime_sweep` when the mission is to find every emitter once.** On
`medium` and `hard` it improves intercept time by 11–20% and cuts
never-intercepted, at the cost of collection depth. Set it with:

```bash
--set agents.phase_locked.fallback=coprime_sweep
```

This is the density pattern from `tier_policy.md` again: as emitter density
rises, the optimum shifts from exploitation toward coverage.
